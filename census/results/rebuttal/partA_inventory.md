# Part A: evidence inventory since the submission tag (read-only; for the ICLR rebuttal)

**Summary (five lines).**
1. **Holds outside the width-one sine network** (registered, OpenTimestamps proof before the run, and strictly causal): forecasts of the switch step and the signed lag, made from the output-scale path before a cutoff. They passed at width-1 GELU (1C G, n 79), width-2 sine (1C T n 101, T′ n 120) and width-4 sine (Phase 3 Q n 112, S n 119, T4 n 95). The delayed crossing at the point where the occupied solution disappears passed on the tanh simplicity-bias network (Phase 2A, 22 rates, one dataset). The output-learning-rate lever passed on the same benchmark at matched scale and loss (Phase 2B, n 80, about 12 times more steps).
2. **Does not hold outside it:** in a standard ReLU MLP (MNIST-CIFAR dominoes) the output-layer lever does nothing (Track L: R FAIL, N PASS, as predicted). LayerNorm does not change this (Track LN, explored negative). Grokking is not covered (G1). The per-seed fold test FAILED on its class rule H (2A-PS2), after a first attempt that was UNRESOLVED on validity (2A-PS).
3. **Inside the width-one sine network:** causal Adam at a = 1.77 PASSES (Track A, n 98, thin C3 margin) and 1C W1 PASSES (n 84, about 17% bias inside a band set after that bias was seen). Test 2A's PASS and Track A's per-run claim at a = 1.65 do NOT hold as causal forecasts (Phase 1B). The registered GELU-T and W2-A passes read the run at or after the crossing. They are supported causally only by the post hoc re-score (1B) and by the registered replication 1C.
4. **Theory:** proved: §14 (F), §15 (L1, L2; fixed-P GD only), §16 (G, ε ≤ 1.4·10⁻¹², s-form), §17/§21 (width-1 compactness) and §22 (width-2 on the continuous windows). U1 (§20, ε₀ = 4.1·10⁻⁶) is proved only given an outer exclusion that is float-certified and was **never Arb-checked**. U2 over ε ∈ [0, 0.6] (1,080 units) is **PRODUCER ONLY, NOT INDEPENDENTLY CHECKED**: only 5 units passed the checker, locally, and the cluster verify-back never ran. So equation (5) stays scoped to the branch.
5. **What limits every pass:** the output-layer updates are slowed (ρ = 2⁻¹⁰ … 2⁻¹⁸), or the runs start from a declared warm start or a branch point. This is not standard training: in standard width-2 training only 12% of crossings are in the law's regime (Phase 2C). The forecast cutoff f = 0.95 was chosen after the pilot: at f = 0.90 the step criterion C1 fails for G, T and T′. Phase 2A/2B each use one fixed dataset.

## List 1: passes outside the width-one sine network that are registered (proof before run) AND strictly causal

Not empty. Six registered tests qualify, with nine arm-level passes. Phase 2B is included with a flag: it makes no forecast, so the causality rule has nothing to read; its prediction was fixed before any registered run. **Excluded on purpose:** the registered GELU-T and W2-A passes (they read the run at or after the crossing; only the post hoc 1B re-score is causal); W2-A D (no declared cutoff; no tolerance); Test 2C (width-1 sine unit, not audited, P at t_sw); and every width-1 sine pass (1C W1, Track A at 1.77).

Terms used below. **Output scale**: the size of the output-layer weights. **Fixed-scale minimiser**: the best network when the output scale is held fixed. **Switch scale**: the output scale at which the fixed-scale minimiser changes from one solution to another. **Placed**: the network has made that switch. **Lag**: how far past (or before) the switch scale training actually switches. **Growth-to-relaxation ratio**: how fast the output scale grows, divided by how fast the hidden weights relax.

1. **Phase 1C, arm G (width-1 GELU).** (Registration 6433edc, proof f458c4c; results 6522a15.) A one-hidden-unit GELU network was trained with plain full-batch SGD on a 1-D classification task, each seed with its own 200-point sample. The output scale was held at half the switch scale, then released. Before training, for each of 80 fresh seeds, a frozen forecaster was fixed. It sees only the output-scale path up to a cutoff: the first step at which the output scale reaches 95% of that seed's switch scale, which was frozen before training. From that path it predicts the step at which the network becomes placed and the signed lag. Outcome: the forecast step was within 10 steps of the observed step in 74 of 79 runs, and the lag within 10 steps in 74 of 79. The median observed/forecast lag ratio was 0.995, and the forecast typically looked 681 steps ahead (median). n = 79. Limits: the 95% cutoff was chosen after pilot seeds. With a 90% cutoff the step criterion fails (9/79 within tolerance). The start is a declared warm start, not standard initialisation.

2. **Phase 1C, arm T (width-2 sine, slowed output layer).** (Same registration.) A two-hidden-unit network with the sine-family activation, on asymmetric windows. Plain SGD with the output-layer learning rate divided by 1,024. Same forecaster, same 95% cutoff. Outcome: step and lag within tolerance (15 and 5 steps) in 101 of 101 scored runs; median lag ratio 0.979; n = 101. Only 101 of 200 seeds reached the targeted solution at release. At a 90% cutoff the step criterion fails (0/101) and the forecast is worse than ignoring the lag.

3. **Phase 1C, arm T′ (width-2 sine, start at a solution, negative lag).** (Same registration.) As T, with the output-layer learning rate divided by 4,096, and each run started exactly on a solution whose lag is negative: training switches before the switch scale. Outcome: step and lag within tolerance in 120 of 120 runs; median lag ratio 1.016; the predicted sign was right in 120 of 120; n = 120. This is a mechanism test from a fixed starting solution. At a 90% cutoff the step criterion fails (0/120).

4. **Phase 2A (tanh simplicity-bias network; delayed switch at the point where the occupied solution disappears).** (Registration 569b836, proof 45fcaed; results 7b15d9f.) The network is tanh with 4 hidden units (3 active), trained by full-batch gradient descent on one fixed 800-point 2-D dataset. One input feature is linear and simple; the other (a slab) is more complex. Only the growth of the output scale was slowed, by a factor ρ from 2⁻¹⁵·¹²⁵ to 2⁻¹⁸. Each run started on the local solution that uses the linear feature. Before training it was predicted that a run does not switch to the slab feature at the switch scale. It switches only when its own local solution stops existing, at 1.33 times the switch scale, crossing between that point and 1.25 times it. The delay past that point was predicted to grow as ρ^{2/3} (registered band 0.55–0.80 for the exponent), and a forecaster reading only the output scale before a cutoff predicted each crossing step. Outcome: all 22 scored rates crossed at 1.004–1.015 times that point. Every forecast was within 15% of its delay (max error 0.097), and the fitted exponent was 0.698. n = 22 rates. Each run is deterministic given its rate, so the rates are the replicates. Limits: one dataset, so this says nothing about sample-to-sample variation (the per-seed follow-ups FAIL or are UNRESOLVED; List 2). It uses a scale-only update rule, not a standard optimiser. The exponent's confidence interval is artificially narrow (post hoc).

5. **Phase 2B (simplicity-bias benchmark; output learning rate as a lever).** (Registration 9239902, proof f62b4a5; results 938ddd7.) The 4-unit tanh network on the same dataset, now from standard initialisation with Adam. 80 fresh seeds, 5 arms. Predicted before training: dividing only the output-weight learning rate by 16 makes the network use the slab feature more, and generalise better on shifted test sets, when compared at the same output scale and at the same training loss. Slab use should start only above the switch scale. A global learning-rate reduction of similar cost should give no gain. Outcome: at every matched point the lower end of the 95% interval was above 0 (smallest +0.118 at matched scale and +0.031 in shifted accuracy at matched loss). Onset was above the switch scale in 77 of 80 runs. The global arms showed no gain beyond 0.02. n = 80 seeds per arm. Limits: it costs about 12 times more steps to reach 3 times the switch scale. At matched steps and at the end of training, the slowed arm uses the slab less (end of training Δ −0.047, 9 of 80 seeds up; no endpoint advantage claimed). One dataset. This is not a forecast, so strict causality is trivial here.

6. **Phase 3 (width-4 sine networks), arms Q, S and T4.** (Registration 7a9d791, proof 16a46bb; results 5a3cb7a.) Four hidden units with the sine-family activation, on asymmetric windows, with the output-layer learning rate divided by 2¹⁰–2¹⁴. The same strictly causal forecaster at a 95% cutoff. Q started each run on a genuinely four-unit solution; S started on a solution with negative lag; T4 started from random holds. Outcome: Q had the step within tolerance in 110 of 112 runs, the lag in 107 of 112, median lag ratio 1.001 (n = 112). S had 119/119 and 117/119, the sign right in 110 of 115, ratio 1.030 (n = 119). T4 had 95/95, ratio 1.007 (n = 95). Limits: random starts never reached a genuinely four-unit solution (0 of 710), so Q and S are mechanism tests from a chosen starting solution, and T4's runs compute width-2 functions. Q's step tolerance (125 steps) is larger than its median predicted lag (87 steps); its lag tolerance is 5 steps.
